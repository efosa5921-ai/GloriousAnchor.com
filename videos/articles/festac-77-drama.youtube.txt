TITLE
FESTAC 77: The Lease | A dramatisation of the four weeks Nigeria was leased to darkness, and the jubilee of 2027

DESCRIPTION
Lagos, 1974. A civil servant holds up a one-naira note and tells his son it buys a dollar and fifty cents in London. Nigeria has just lent money to the IMF and the World Bank. Two years later he is seconded to the secretariat of FESTAC 77, and his wife signs a letter from a small Pentecostal fellowship calling the festival "a plot of the devil to revive the worship of idols". On 15 January 1977 he takes the boy to the National Stadium to watch a priest of Shango light the festival flame.

This short film follows the Oyelaran family from that night to today, and looks ahead to the morning of 12 February 2027, fifty years to the day after the festival closed at the Durbar in Kaduna. It dramatises what was done in Nigeria's name, what followed, why the fifty-year lease ends on 12 February 2027, why the 2027 elections were moved inside the lease's last month, why by God's jubilee in Leviticus 25:9-15 (release of slaves and captives, cancellation of debt, return of property, rest for the land) the powers will no longer have grounds to stand, and the two jubilees the nation must choose between.
