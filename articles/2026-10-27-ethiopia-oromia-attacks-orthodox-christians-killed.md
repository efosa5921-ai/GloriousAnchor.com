> *They went from house to house.* That is how survivors describe it, and it is how the Bible describes the night of judgment in Egypt, except that here there was no blood on the doorposts to keep death out.

In the space of five days, armed men killed at least sixty Orthodox Christians in two districts of Ethiopia's Oromia region, the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said on Monday 26 October. Fourteen died on 20 October in Merti district; forty-six more on the night of 24 October in three villages of Shirka district. Both are in the Arsi Zone of central Ethiopia. More than twenty people were seriously wounded, twenty homes were burned, and five of the dead, eyewitnesses say, were children.

The story was carried by Reuters, the Associated Press, the BBC, Al Jazeera and Vatican News. It was not mentioned by the leaders of Britain, France, Spain, Italy, Germany, Ireland, Norway or Canada, a silence that one widely shared post on X set out name by name. Here is what is known.

## What happened in Merti and Shirka

The church's Holy Synod gave the details. On 20 October, attackers killed fourteen people at Gora Silingo, in Angada kebele of Merti district. On 24 October, in the villages of Tereta, Chole and Birbo in Shirka district, they killed forty-six.

Survivors told the BBC's Amharic service and Vatican News how the second attack unfolded. It came overnight, between Saturday and Sunday. "We were completely unprepared and had no prior knowledge," said a resident whose 75-year-old father was killed. "They surrounded us from all four directions." Witnesses said the assailants went from house to house, killing with gunfire and knives. The elderly and children were among the dead. One church was reportedly damaged, and churches in neighbouring villages sheltered those who fled. A church official said security forces arrived but could not stop the assault.

The area is home to Muslims, Protestants and Orthodox Christians together. The dead were Orthodox.

## What the Ethiopian Orthodox Church said

The Holy Synod called the killings "brutal and merciless massacres" and spoke of "extremely tragic cruelty and suffering inflicted on our citizens". It said the attacks violated the constitution's protection of life and freedom of religion and threatened Ethiopia's tradition of peaceful coexistence among faiths, and it warned that attacks on Christians have increased in recent months despite its repeated appeals to officials at every level of government.

The Synod called on the authorities to condemn the killings publicly, to protect civilians and to bring the killers to justice, and said it would seek talks with senior officials. It held prayers for the victims on Monday and directed Orthodox churches and monasteries around the world to pray for them after the Divine Liturgy on Tuesday.

## Who is responsible

Nobody has claimed the attacks. The Oromia regional government's spokesman, Hailu Adugna, blamed the Oromo Liberation Army (OLA), accusing the rebels and their allies of trying to provoke ethnic and religious conflict to distract from military losses in the north. He gave no evidence. The OLA, which the government lists as a terrorist organisation, could not be reached; its leader, Kumsa Diriba, known as Jaal Marroo, told the Associated Press in February that "our war is not against the people", though United Nations investigators have accused the group of killings, rapes and kidnappings.

The OLA is one of seven groups in a rebel alliance formed in September to remove Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government, alongside the Tigray People's Liberation Front and the Amhara Fano movement. The Ethiopian Human Rights Council, a non-governmental body, reported in July that more than 430 civilians had been killed in targeted attacks across East Arsi since 2021, many of them members of the Orthodox Church, in attacks it described as religiously motivated. Eleven people were killed in a similar attack in the same area in May and June.

Some outlets have called this an Islamist attack. That claim rests on an anonymous social media post, and none of the agencies reporting from the ground, nor the church itself, has made it. Oromia's conflict has ethnic and religious strands tangled together, and Muslims in the region have been attacked too. What is established is that the dead were Orthodox Christians, that the church says they were targeted as such, and that nobody has yet been held to account.

Verifying anything in Oromia is hard. Amnesty International's researchers say internet blackouts and restrictions on independent media have hidden the extent of civilian suffering there, under both the rebels and government forces: "What we have been able to document now is just the tip of the iceberg."

## Questions this article answers

### What happened in Ethiopia's Oromia region in October 2026?

Armed men killed at least sixty Orthodox Christians in two attacks: fourteen on 20 October at Gora Silingo in Merti district, and forty-six on the night of 24 October in the villages of Tereta, Chole and Birbo in Shirka district, both in the Arsi Zone. More than twenty were wounded and twenty homes burned.

### Who carried out the attacks on Christians in Oromia?

Nobody has claimed them. The Oromia regional government blamed the Oromo Liberation Army and its rebel allies without presenting evidence; the OLA has not responded but has previously denied targeting civilians. Claims that the attackers were Islamists rest on an anonymous social media post and are not made by the church or by agencies reporting from Ethiopia.

### What did the Ethiopian Orthodox Church say?

The Holy Synod called the killings "brutal and merciless massacres", said they violated constitutional protections for life and religious freedom, warned that attacks on Christians have increased, and called on the government to condemn the killings, protect civilians and bring the killers to justice. It ordered prayers for the victims in Orthodox churches worldwide.

### Have Western leaders responded to the killing of Christians in Ethiopia?

As of 27 October, no statement from the leaders of Britain, France, Spain, Italy, Germany, Ireland, Norway or Canada had been reported, nor one from Ethiopia's federal government. A post listing their silence spread widely on X.

### Why are Orthodox Christians being attacked in Oromia?

Oromia has been at war between government forces and the OLA since 2018, with other armed groups also active. The Ethiopian Human Rights Council documented more than 430 civilians killed in targeted attacks in East Arsi since 2021, many of them Orthodox Christians, in attacks it called religiously motivated; ethnic and religious grievances are intertwined, and Muslims have also been attacked.
