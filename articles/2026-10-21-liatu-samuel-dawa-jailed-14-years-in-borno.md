> *Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body.* (Hebrews 13:3)

A Christian mother in Borno State, north-east Nigeria, is serving a 14-year prison sentence because a Muslim woman she spoke to about Jesus decided to follow him. Her name is Liatu Samuel Dawa (some reports spell it Liyatu). Her husband, an evangelist, is in hiding. Here is what has been reported so far, what the charge actually was, and how the church can respond.

*Photo: Liatu Samuel Dawa, shared by her husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman.*

## What happened to Liatu Samuel Dawa

According to [Truth Nigeria](https://truthnigeria.com/2026/10/nigerian-court-sentences-christian-woman-to-14-years-in-prison-for-sharing-her-faith/), which obtained a certified copy of the judgment, Liatu shared her faith with an adult Muslim woman in Borno State. The woman later chose to become a Christian. At the trial she testified that nobody forced her and that she acted of her own free will.

That testimony did not save Liatu. Hon. Justice A. B. Kumdila of the Borno State High Court convicted her and imposed the maximum penalty: 14 years' imprisonment on Count 1, with no option of a fine.

[Starconnect Media](https://starconnectmedia.com/christian-mother-liatu-samuel-sentenced-to-14-years-in-prison-over-evangelism-in-borno/) reported on 20 October that public outrage and calls for judicial intervention have grown since the sentence became known. The story reached a wider audience through [a post on X](https://x.com/SpiricocoNg/status/2112464727108428111) that we also feature in our [News](/news/) section. No international wire service has covered the case yet.

## The charge: Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code

This is the detail that has lawyers alarmed. Liatu was not convicted under any law that mentions preaching or religion. Truth Nigeria reports that Count 1 was brought under Section 210 of the Penal Code Laws of Borno State, a provision about depriving a parent of the lawful custody of a child.

The woman who converted is an adult. Human-rights lawyer Samuel John told Truth Nigeria that the conviction has no constitutional basis: "No provision of Nigerian law justifies a 14-year sentence for a consensual religious conversation between two adults." He called the judgment "unconstitutional and deeply troubling."

Gavvs Katiya, chairman of the Gwoza Refugees Resettlement Initiative, said the ruling "weaponized her Christian conviction" and imposed a punishment far beyond anything Nigerian law justifies.

Liatu's husband goes further, alleging that the court applied a religious reading of the case rather than the statute. We cannot verify that from the judgment; we report it as his claim.

## "I only advised her": Liatu's plea to the court

Before sentencing, Liatu asked the judge for leniency. The certified judgment records her words:

> "I'm a mother. I did not intend to cheat the victim. I only advised her. That is all."

The plea was rejected.

## Her husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman, in hiding

Liatu's husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman, told Truth Nigeria he spent months in detention himself before securing bail. Since his release, he says, local extremists have been hunting for him, and he now lives in hiding. The threats have cut him off from his wife; he cannot visit her in prison without risking his life.

"I'm on the run; my life is under threat," he said. "My wife is a good person. The young woman they claim she coerced is an adult. She testified clearly that no one forced her to accept Christ. Yet my wife is in prison suffering."

He has appealed to international human-rights organisations, religious-freedom advocates and world leaders: "I'm appealing to the world to save my life and my wife."

## What Nigeria's Constitution says about freedom of religion

Section 38(1) of the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria guarantees every person "freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including freedom to change his religion or belief, and freedom (either alone or in community with others, and in public or in private) to manifest and propagate his religion or belief in worship, teaching, practice and observance."

Two freedoms in that sentence meet in this case: the convert's freedom to change her religion, and Liatu's freedom to propagate hers through teaching. Rights advocates quoted in both reports say the sentence breaches both.

Nigeria is also a party to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, whose Article 8 guarantees freedom of conscience and the free practice of religion.

## Questions this article answers

### Who is Liatu Samuel Dawa?

Liatu (also spelled Liyatu) Samuel Dawa is a Christian mother from Borno State in north-east Nigeria. She is married to Evangelist Amos Suleman. In October 2026 Nigerian outlets reported that she had been sentenced to 14 years in prison after a Muslim woman she shared her faith with became a Christian.

### Why was Liatu Samuel Dawa sentenced to 14 years in prison?

She shared her faith with an adult Muslim woman who then converted to Christianity. The woman testified that she acted freely. Justice A. B. Kumdila of the Borno State High Court nevertheless convicted Liatu under Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code, a provision about depriving a parent of custody of a child, and imposed the maximum 14-year term with no option of a fine.

### Is it illegal to share your faith in Nigeria?

No. Section 38 of Nigeria's 1999 Constitution guarantees freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including the freedom to change religion and to propagate it through teaching. Lawyers quoted in the reports say no Nigerian law justifies a 14-year sentence for a religious conversation between two consenting adults.

Evangelist Amos Suleman says he was detained for months before being released on bail. He reports death threats from local extremists and now lives in hiding, unable to visit his wife in prison. He has appealed to international organisations for help.
