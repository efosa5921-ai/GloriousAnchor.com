> *Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body.* (Hebrews 13:3)

A Christian mother in Borno State, north-east Nigeria, is serving a 14-year prison sentence after a Muslim girl she spoke to about Jesus decided to follow him. Her name is Liatu Samuel Dawa (some reports spell it Liyatu). Her husband, an evangelist, is in hiding. Here is what has been reported so far, what the charge actually was, and how the church can respond.

*Photo: Liatu Samuel Dawa, shared by her husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman.*

**Correction and update, Wednesday 4 November.** This article was written on 21 October from the first two reports of the case, which said Liatu had shared her faith with an adult Muslim woman. Fuller accounts of the court record have since been published, and they change the picture, so we set them out here rather than quietly rewrite what we said. The case is *Lami Samuel Dawa and four others*, suit BOHC/MG/CR/154/2025, before Justice A. B. Kumdila at the Borno State High Court in Maiduguri; judgment was delivered on 5 May 2026, not in October, and the four co-accused were discharged and acquitted. The convert who testified is named in court-related accounts as Aisha Kabir Saleh, who was 16 when she gave evidence and said she became a Christian at 13. The charge sheet had six counts, including conspiracy to abduct a child, enticing a child from parental custody, removing a child from lawful custody, depriving the child of schooling and of practising her religion, and screening an offender; the prosecution alleged she was moved from her family home in Maiduguri to Christian institutions in Jos. The conviction was on Count 1, under Section 210 of the Borno Penal Code, as we reported. The Christian Lawyers' Fellowship of Nigeria, which defended Liatu at trial, says the case "centred on child custody" and has cautioned against presenting it as a conviction "simply for preaching to a girl or converting her"; its Maiduguri chairman, Ephraim Yidawi, confirms that an appeal has been filed. The Pentecostal Fellowship of Nigeria and CLASFON jointly urged Christians to establish the full facts before drawing conclusions. The Christian Association of Nigeria's president, Archbishop Daniel Okoh, said on 31 October that CAN has "deep concern" about the sentence, that "an appeal has already been filed", and that "the precise facts and legal basis of the conviction are still undergoing verification". Borno's commissioner for information, Usman Tar, called it "a technical legal matter". The Muslim Public Affairs Centre has accused CAN of an inaccurate narrative and asked the state to protect the girl's family. What has not changed: Liatu is serving fourteen years with no option of a fine, the maximum the section allows, for what her lawyers and the churches say was at most a custody matter involving a girl who testified that she chose Christ freely; her husband remains in hiding; and the appeal is the next step. We have corrected the sections below where the first reports were wrong, and we will report the appeal.

## What happened to Liatu Samuel Dawa

According to [Truth Nigeria](https://truthnigeria.com/2026/10/nigerian-court-sentences-christian-woman-to-14-years-in-prison-for-sharing-her-faith/), which obtained a certified copy of the judgment, Liatu shared her faith with a Muslim woman in Borno State who later chose to become a Christian, and who testified at the trial that nobody forced her and that she acted of her own free will. Truth Nigeria described her as an adult; the court record, as later reported, gives her age as 13 at conversion and 16 when she testified (see the correction above).

That testimony did not save Liatu. Hon. Justice A. B. Kumdila of the Borno State High Court convicted her and imposed the maximum penalty: 14 years' imprisonment on Count 1, with no option of a fine.

[Starconnect Media](https://starconnectmedia.com/christian-mother-liatu-samuel-sentenced-to-14-years-in-prison-over-evangelism-in-borno/) reported on 20 October that public outrage and calls for judicial intervention have grown since the sentence became known. The story reached a wider audience through [a post on X](https://x.com/SpiricocoNg/status/2112464727108428111) that we also feature in our [News](/news/) section. No international wire service has covered the case yet.

## The charge: Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code

This is the detail that has lawyers alarmed. Liatu was not convicted under any law that mentions preaching or religion. Truth Nigeria reports that Count 1 was brought under Section 210 of the Penal Code Laws of Borno State, a provision about depriving a parent of the lawful custody of a child.

Human-rights lawyer Samuel John told Truth Nigeria that the conviction has no constitutional basis: "No provision of Nigerian law justifies a 14-year sentence for a consensual religious conversation between two adults." He called the judgment "unconstitutional and deeply troubling."

Gavvs Katiya, chairman of the Gwoza Refugees Resettlement Initiative, said the ruling "weaponized her Christian conviction" and imposed a punishment far beyond anything Nigerian law justifies.

Liatu's husband goes further, alleging that the court applied a religious reading of the case rather than the statute. We cannot verify that from the judgment; we report it as his claim.

## "I only advised her": Liatu's plea to the court

Before sentencing, Liatu asked the judge for leniency. The certified judgment records her words:

> "I'm a mother. I did not intend to cheat the victim. I only advised her. That is all."

The plea was rejected.

## Her husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman, in hiding

Liatu's husband, Evangelist Amos Suleman, told Truth Nigeria he spent months in detention himself before securing bail. Since his release, he says, local extremists have been hunting for him, and he now lives in hiding. The threats have cut him off from his wife; he cannot visit her in prison without risking his life.

"I'm on the run; my life is under threat," he said. "My wife is a good person. The young woman they claim she coerced testified clearly that no one forced her to accept Christ. Yet my wife is in prison suffering."

He has appealed to international human-rights organisations, religious-freedom advocates and world leaders: "I'm appealing to the world to save my life and my wife."

## What Nigeria's Constitution says about freedom of religion

Section 38(1) of the 1999 Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria guarantees every person "freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including freedom to change his religion or belief, and freedom (either alone or in community with others, and in public or in private) to manifest and propagate his religion or belief in worship, teaching, practice and observance."

Two freedoms in that sentence meet in this case: the convert's freedom to change her religion, and Liatu's freedom to propagate hers through teaching. Rights advocates quoted in both reports say the sentence breaches both.

Nigeria is also a party to the African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, whose Article 8 guarantees freedom of conscience and the free practice of religion.

## Questions this article answers

### Who is Liatu Samuel Dawa?

Liatu (also spelled Liyatu) Samuel Dawa is a Christian mother from Borno State in north-east Nigeria. She is married to Evangelist Amos Suleman. In October 2026 Nigerian outlets reported that she had been sentenced, in May 2026, to 14 years in prison after a Muslim girl she shared her faith with became a Christian; her lawyers have appealed.

### Why was Liatu Samuel Dawa sentenced to 14 years in prison?

She was convicted of depriving a parent of the custody of a child: a Muslim girl who, by the court record, became a Christian at 13 and testified at 16 that she acted freely. The prosecution alleged the girl was moved from Maiduguri to Christian institutions in Jos. Justice A. B. Kumdila of the Borno State High Court nevertheless convicted Liatu under Section 210 of the Borno State Penal Code, a provision about depriving a parent of custody of a child, and imposed the maximum 14-year term with no option of a fine.

### Is it illegal to share your faith in Nigeria?

No. Section 38 of Nigeria's 1999 Constitution guarantees freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including the freedom to change religion and to propagate it through teaching. Lawyers quoted in the first reports said no Nigerian law justifies a 14-year sentence for a religious conversation; the Christian Lawyers' Fellowship, which defended her, says the case turned on child custody and is on appeal.

### What happened to Liatu Samuel Dawa's husband?

Evangelist Amos Suleman says he was detained for months before being released on bail. He reports death threats from local extremists and now lives in hiding, unable to visit his wife in prison. He has appealed to international organisations for help.

### How can Christians help Liatu Samuel Dawa?

Pray for her, her husband and the woman who believed; share the story so the case gets wider attention; and support organisations that document and assist persecuted Christians in Nigeria. Hebrews 13:3 asks believers to remember those in prison as though imprisoned with them.
