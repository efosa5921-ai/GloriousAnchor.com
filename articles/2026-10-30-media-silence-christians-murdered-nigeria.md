> *Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body.* (Hebrews 13:3)

"When Christians are murdered in Nigeria, the media goes silent." That is how Congresswoman Nancy Mace of South Carolina opened a video posted to X on Thursday 29 October 2026, over footage of burned churches and displaced families. "Churches are being burned. Families are being driven from their homes. More than 5 million Christians were forced to flee. God protect our people."

Her post has been shared tens of thousands of times, and many of our readers in Nigeria and the diaspora will recognise the frustration behind it. But is it true? Does the world's press really ignore the killing of Christians in Nigeria, and if so, why? This article tests the claim against the record: the death tolls, a specific massacre that became the test case, the way the violence is described, the reasons newsrooms give, and what the past year, in which an American president took up the cause, has changed. It closes with what a Christian should do about it.

## The numbers behind the claim

Nobody disputes that Nigeria is the deadliest country in the world in which to be a Christian. The question is how deadly, and the figures depend on who is counting.

- **Open Doors**, whose annual World Watch List is the most cited measure, counted 4,849 Christians killed for their faith worldwide in its 2026 reporting year, 3,490 of them in Nigeria, about 72 per cent of the global total. The year before it was 3,100 of 4,476.
- **Intersociety**, a Nigerian civil-society group based in Onitsha, counts higher and faster: 3,083 Christians killed and 3,193 abducted between January and August 2026, rising to 3,426 killed by 10 October. Its figures are compiled from local reports and are not independently verified, and its methods have been questioned.
- **Aid to the Church in Need**, the Catholic charity, published its "Persecuted and Forgotten?" report on 27 October 2026. It says attacks by jihadists and radicalised Fulani militias in the Middle Belt have helped displace more than 3.5 million people inside Nigeria, at least a million of them Christians.
- **Amnesty International** counted 10,217 people of all faiths killed by armed groups in Nigeria over two years to 2025.
- **United Nations experts** warned in June 2026 of "credible reports of killings, abductions, sexual violence, forced conversion targeting Christians" in Nigeria.

Mace's own figures, used in earlier posts, are that 5 million Christians have been displaced, 9,100 churches destroyed, more than 850 Christians held hostage and 600 clergy attacked. The displacement number is higher than ACN's; it appears to come from older estimates that count everyone uprooted in the north and Middle Belt since the Boko Haram insurgency began. The point stands whichever set you use: thousands of Christians a year are being killed because of their faith, and a million or more have lost their homes.

## The test case: Yelwata

On the night of Friday 13 June 2025 a "killer squad" of armed men descended on Yelwata, a town in Guma local government area of Benue State where displaced Christians from earlier attacks had taken shelter in and around a Catholic mission. For about three hours they shot, hacked and burned. The diocese of Makurdi's roll of the dead stands at 271 names: 67 children, 83 women and 122 men. It was one of the worst single massacres of Christians anywhere in the world this decade.

Madeleine Kearns, writing in The Free Press under the headline "As Christians Are Slaughtered, the World Looks Away", found that in the days afterwards the major American and British outlets barely mentioned it, and those that did described it as a clash between farmers and herders. The Catholic Bishop of Makurdi, Wilfred Anagbe, told a United States House subcommittee that the Nigerian government had abandoned the survivors and was downplaying "an escalating crisis of Christian persecution and genocide". A year on, in June 2026, he said a memorial Mass for the 271 and asked again why nobody had been arrested.

Yelwata is the reason Bill Maher, who is not a Christian, told his HBO audience on 26 September 2025: "They are systematically killing the Christians in Nigeria. If you don't know what's going on in Nigeria, your media sources suck. You are in a bubble." Mace thanked him for "breaking the silence".

## Is the silence real?

Partly. Two things are true at once, and honest Christians should hold both.

First, the big attacks do get reported. When gunmen killed at least 62 people in Binnari, Taraba State, on 24 October this year, the Associated Press, Reuters and Al Jazeera all carried it within a day. Glenn Beck's video on that attack, which we [featured this week](/picks/), was itself a reaction to wire coverage. The Media Research Center has tracked American network news on Nigeria since 2016 and found long stretches, in 2016, 2019 and again in 2025, when ABC, CBS and NBC aired nothing on massacres of Christians while covering lesser Nigerian stories; but the same group's complaint in November 2025 was not silence, it was that the networks had started covering the story only because Donald Trump made it news, and then framed it as a conservative talking point.

Second, what Christians in Nigeria experience as silence is better described as three habits of coverage:

- **The frame.** Attacks are reported as "clashes between farmers and herders", "communal violence" or "banditry", with the religion of the dead left out. The Binnari report that reached most of the world called the attackers "bandits" and never said who the victims were. The Middle Belt Concern, a coalition of more than 80 civic groups, calls those labels "misleading"; ACN's new report says church attacks are "presented primarily in terms of ethnic conflict, competition over land and water resources", and the Vatican's observer at the United Nations, Archbishop Ettore Balestrero, has said hostility toward Christians is "frequently overlooked in international media coverage". Truth Nigeria, a Christian outlet in Abuja, published a study in September titled "How Nigeria's Media Rewrites Massacres into 'Clashes'", tracing how the softer wording in Nigerian papers is then copied by foreign ones.
- **The follow-up.** A massacre gets a day; the funerals, the camps, the absence of arrests and the next attack on the same village do not. Yelwata's 271 dead have produced no prosecutions and almost no foreign reporting since the first week.
- **The scepticism.** When advocates say "genocide", much of the press responds by fact-checking the word rather than reporting the killings. CNN's Christmas Day 2025 explainer was headlined "Trump says violence in Nigeria targets Christians. The reality is more nuanced"; Al Jazeera ran "No, Bill Maher, there is no 'Christian genocide' in Nigeria"; Newsweek noted that more Muslims than Christians have died in terrorist attacks nationally. Those pieces are not wrong to say the picture is complex. They are wrong if complexity becomes a reason to say less.

## Why newsrooms go quiet

The reasons are worth understanding, because some of them are legitimate and some are not.

The violence genuinely is complicated. Boko Haram and ISWAP kill Muslims who refuse them as readily as Christians; "bandits" in the north-west kidnap for ransom regardless of faith; in the Middle Belt the attackers are mostly Fulani Muslim herders and the victims mostly Christian farmers, but land, cattle and politics are tangled up with religion. Nigeria's government insists on the complicated version. President Tinubu said in December 2025 that the crisis is "rooted in historical, economic and criminal issues, not religious issues", his information minister called the genocide label "a gross misrepresentation of reality", and a presidential adviser called it "a hoax". Foreign correspondents who rely on Abuja for access and official figures tend to adopt Abuja's vocabulary.

The numbers are contested, and that hurts the story. Intersociety's counts are not independently verified; Maher's claim of 100,000 dead since 2009 had no clear source; Mace's 5 million displaced is at the high end. Every inflated figure gives a sceptical editor a reason to spike the piece.

Access is hard and dangerous. Benue, Plateau and Kaduna are far from Lagos and Abuja bureaus, and the men who do the killing have also killed journalists. Most of what the world knows comes from Nigerian Christian reporters and from Morning Star News, Truth Nigeria and the Catholic agencies, which the big outlets treat as advocacy rather than news.

And the story has been politically coded. Since Senator Ted Cruz, Congresswoman Mace and then President Trump took it up in autumn 2025, it has been filed in many newsrooms under American culture war rather than African mass killing. That is a failure of judgement, not a reason, but it is real.

## What changed when Washington spoke

On 31 October 2025, after months of pressure from members of Congress including Mace, President Trump declared that "Christianity is facing an existential threat in Nigeria" and ordered the State Department to designate the country a Country of Particular Concern for religious freedom violations. He threatened to cut aid and told the Pentagon to "prepare for possible action". On Christmas Day 2025 the United States struck Islamic State positions in north-west Nigeria, strikes Trump called "numerous" and "perfect", and in January 2026 he said there could be more. Nigeria, which had denied any genocide, said it would "welcome American support", and the two militaries have since deepened cooperation.

Coverage spiked, then subsided. The attacks did not. Morning Star News counted six Christians killed in Plateau State on 3 and 4 October 2026, three more in Benue on 7 October and one in Kaduna on 1 October; Truth Nigeria reported twelve dead in Benue the same week; Binnari followed on 24 October. In July 2026 the Catholic and Protestant bishops of northern Nigeria made a joint statement that Truth Nigeria called a break with "decades of silence", with the northern chairman of the Christian Association of Nigeria, Joseph Hayab, admitting that many church leaders "have been intimidated into silence". The silence Mace describes is not only in New York and London.

## How a Christian should respond

Scripture does not let us look away. "Remember those who are in prison, as though in prison with them, and those who are mistreated, since you also are in the body" (Hebrews 13:3). "If one member suffers, all suffer together" (1 Corinthians 12:26). The believer in Lagos or London who does not know what happened at Yelwata is, in Paul's terms, a limb that has stopped feeling.

So, first, know the facts and tell them. "Open your mouth for the mute, for the rights of all who are destitute" (Proverbs 31:8). Follow the outlets that do the reporting, share their work, and name the dead: Yelwata, Binnari, Riyom, Otukpo, Mangu. A story the press will not tell is a story the Church must.

Second, tell the truth, including the inconvenient parts. "A false balance is an abomination to the LORD" (Proverbs 11:1) cuts both ways. Christians who repeat unverified numbers, or who ignore the Muslims also killed by the same jihadists, hand the sceptics their excuse. Use Open Doors' figures, say where the others come from, and let the verified truth be terrible enough.

Third, pray and give. Pray for the bereaved and displaced, for the pastors and bishops who are threatened, for the Nigerian soldiers and police who stand between villages and their attackers, and for the journalists who go where others will not. Give to the charities on the ground, such as Aid to the Church in Need, Open Doors and the dioceses that run the camps.

And fourth, do not despair of the silence. "Shall not God give justice to his elect, who cry to him day and night?" (Luke 18:7). The Lord who saw Abel's blood from the ground (Genesis 4:10) sees Benue's. Our job is to make sure that when the world finally looks, the record is there.

## Questions this article answers

### What did Nancy Mace say about Christians in Nigeria?

In a video on X on 29 October 2026 she said: "When Christians are murdered in Nigeria, the media goes silent. Churches are being burned. Families are being driven from their homes. More than 5 million Christians were forced to flee. God protect our people." In earlier posts she has cited 9,100 churches destroyed, 850 hostages and 600 clergy attacked.

### How many Christians are killed in Nigeria each year?

Open Doors counted 3,490 Christians killed for their faith in Nigeria in its 2026 reporting year, 72 per cent of the world total. Intersociety, a Nigerian group whose figures are not independently verified, counts 3,426 from 1 January to 10 October 2026. Aid to the Church in Need says more than 3.5 million people are displaced in Nigeria, at least a million of them Christians.

### Does the mainstream media ignore the killing of Christians in Nigeria?

Not entirely: wire services report the largest attacks, as they did for Binnari in October 2026. But coverage often omits the victims' faith, uses the "farmer-herder clashes" frame, rarely follows up, and since 2025 has treated the subject as a partisan American issue. The June 2025 Yelwata massacre, with 271 dead, drew almost no Western coverage.

### What was the Yelwata massacre?

An attack on the night of 13 June 2025 on Yelwata, Benue State, where displaced Christians sheltered at a Catholic mission. The diocese of Makurdi lists 271 dead, including 67 children. No one has been prosecuted.

### What has the US government done about Christians in Nigeria?

President Trump designated Nigeria a Country of Particular Concern on 31 October 2025, threatened to cut aid, and ordered strikes on Islamic State targets in north-west Nigeria on 25 December 2025. Nigeria denies a genocide but has accepted American security cooperation.

### How should Christians respond to persecution in Nigeria?

Remember the persecuted as members of the same body (Hebrews 13:3, 1 Corinthians 12:26), speak for them with verified facts (Proverbs 31:8, Proverbs 11:1), pray for them, give to the charities that serve them, and trust God's justice (Luke 18:7).
