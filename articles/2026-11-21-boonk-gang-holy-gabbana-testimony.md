> *Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new.* (2 Corinthians 5:17)

In 2017 a twenty-year-old from Florida became one of the most watched people on the internet by filming himself stealing. He would vault a shop counter, grab what he wanted, and run for the door shouting two words that a whole generation of teenagers then shouted after him: "Boonk gang!"

Nine years later the same man preaches Christ. He calls himself Holy Gabbana, and the fifty-six-minute film of his life, *I Am Holy Gabbana*, carries a sentence of his that sums up the years in between: "I gave the world my all, and I went straight to the bottom."

This is his story, told as far as possible in his own words and from the public record, with the parts that are disputed marked as disputed.

## Who was Boonk Gang?

His name is John Robert Hill Jr. He was born in 1996 and comes from Jacksonville, Florida. In 2017 he began posting videos on Instagram under the name Boonk. In the first, he walked behind the counter of a Popeyes restaurant and left with a box of fried chicken. Others showed him running off with sodas, a pizza, a watch, somebody's phone. Within months he had millions of followers: more than four million by one count, five million by his own film's.

He told a Miami television station in July 2017 that none of it was acted. "All my videos are real," he said. "I call it boonkin' because, you know, I'm running off." And he said why he did it: "It's a publicity stunt. So, now that I got up there, now I'm trying to get my music back in."

He has since explained where the idea came from. He had watched another man get rich by behaving badly on camera. "All you got to do to make this much money is to be a fool and act reckless… I could do that," he told the God Behind Bars podcast. "I started being a nut."

The law noticed. After he jumped the counter of a Dunkin' Donuts in Miami Gardens in May 2017 and took a tray of doughnuts worth $38, he was charged with burglary and petit theft. "Try Krispy Kreme, but stay out of this stuff," the judge told him. He was not yet twenty-one.

## The boy behind the videos

What the followers saw was a clown. What they did not see, he says, was the state he was in.

"I gained worldwide fame at 20 years old, but I have so much trauma and brokenness on the inside that the world didn't know about," he told CBN. He has spoken of abuse at home, of going hungry, and of losing at fourteen the stepfather who was the only father he knew. "After he passed away, it was just my mother. She was working and she was really isolated. I was left navigating my teenage years primarily on my own."

Even the fame was thinner than it looked. "A lot of those videos I was homeless and still making videos," he told Holy Culture Radio, "because I believed it would set me free in life." He dulled the pain with pills and became addicted.

A reader should know that his mother has publicly rejected parts of this account. In posts reported by The Shade Room in January 2023 she denied that he was raised in foster care or had to search bins for food, and said he was not telling the full story. We cannot settle that from here, and we set down his account as his account.

## 2018: the fall

The year after he became famous, it came apart.

- **March.** On 7 March 2018, a month after he posted a video of himself holding firearms, Los Angeles County sheriff's deputies searched his home in Calabasas. He was arrested on felony charges of possessing assault weapons and of possessing prescription medication without a prescription, and released on $35,000 bail.
- **July.** On 2 July, Instagram removed his account, and with it the millions of followers, after he posted sexually explicit videos of himself.
- **The same month.** He arrived for a filmed interview so intoxicated that he could hardly speak or stand, and said he had been that way for three weeks. The clip went around the world. His own film calls it the moment the public understood he "was facing a very real problem".

"I gave the world my all, and I went straight to the bottom," he says of that time. "It made me not want to live."

## "Do you believe in Jesus?": the jail cell

By his own account he then faced a hit-and-run charge, three gun charges and a narcotics charge in Los Angeles. "It started out as a three-year prison sentence," he told CBN, "and I end up in a sentence five months county time." He served it in the Los Angeles County Jail.

His cellmate was a man named Joshua. The first thing Joshua asked him was a question.

> "He was like, 'Do you believe in Jesus?' I'm like, 'Yeah.' For that man to ask me, 'do I believe in Jesus?' That lets me know that God was always thinking about me. Anybody else could have been my cellmate. In that moment, God reached me through my cellmate."

He asked Joshua to tell him more. "I just remember this man teaching me the Bible, and more than teaching me, showed me the love of God, and my heart began to transform, because I've done so much wrong in my life. I've caused so much hurt and pain to people."

One night, after reading, he began to sing in the cell, as loudly as he could: "Jesus loves you. He died on the cross for our sins. He's always with you from beginning to the end." Then he lay down on his bunk and wept.

> "I wasn't crying for long. I felt a touch. It wasn't like a hand. It was a touch. It started small but it got bigger. It was so soft, and it felt so comforting."

"Immediately the presence of God came over me and stripped my sadness away and filled me with joy," he said. He jumped down and woke his cellmate to show him the tears on his face. Joshua told him it was the Holy Spirit.

Looking back, he says this of the worst year of his life: "When I went to jail, Jesus wasn't on my mind… You may not be thinking about Jesus, but He is thinking about you. But God was literally setting me up so I could have an encounter with Him."

## Back to the world, and back again

It would be a tidier story if it ended there. It did not, and he does not pretend it did.

"I learned about Jesus when I went to jail, and came out still living a worldly life," he told The Shade Room in 2022. "I didn't have an example, I didn't have no guidance." In the summer of 2019 he was back to making stunt videos, and one of them ended with a punch that broke his jaw and left him needing surgery.

What brought him back, he says, was a friend who came close to death and returned with a testimony of his own. "I got sidetracked and continued to live fleshly and worldly. But years later a brother of mine had his own testimony. One man plants, another man waters, and God gets an increase."

On 14 December 2020 he posted a video to Facebook, in tears:

> "I know why I'm still standing here. I know why I made it so far in life. I know that even when I fail and fall flat on my face, I know why I was able to get back up and fight through it all. I've said it was me, but it wasn't me. It was God."

In it he prayed aloud: "Father, I just want to stand in front of You. I bow down in front of You. I wanna ask that You forgive me. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry." Around that time he said he had given up the stunts and was working for his uncle, remodelling swimming pools. In February 2021 he was baptised.

## Holy Gabbana today

He had already dropped the name Boonk; he became John Gabbana, and then Holy Gabbana. He makes Christian rap, including a song called "Holy Flow", speaks about his life wherever he is asked, and in 2024 told his story on the podcast of God Behind Bars, a ministry to prisoners.

Of the life he left he says: "The money didn't bring happiness. The girls I slept with didn't bring happiness. Views didn't bring happiness. I wanted happiness. I wanted peace. I didn't get past that point until I gave my life to Christ."

His walk since has been lived in public, and it has not been smooth. Besides the dispute with his mother, a former girlfriend made accusations against him online in 2022, which entertainment sites reported and which, as far as we can find, were never tested in a court. He is a young believer with a past that the internet does not forget. That is a reason to pray for him, and not a reason to doubt what God began in a jail cell.

"I'm not ashamed to share my failures with people," he says, "because sharing my failures with somebody could really encourage them. It's never too late to make a turnaround."

## The film: I Am Holy Gabbana

The documentary was directed by Jonathan Williams and produced by Jennifer Pessima, with Dolapo Erinkitola and Uncle Reece as executive producers. It was first shown on 12 August 2022 at the Riverside EpiCenter in Austell, Georgia, near Atlanta, under the title *I Am John Gabbana*, and runs for 56 minutes. It follows him from his childhood in Jacksonville through addiction, fame, the interview that exposed him and the jail. Its makers say that while it was being filmed he forgave the father who had been absent from his life.

It was released [on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HO-ky-Zws4A) in July 2025 as *I Am Holy Gabbana*, where at the time of writing it can be watched only from the United States, and it is listed on other streaming services, among them Tubi and Prime Video. It deals plainly with drugs, violence and abuse, and is not a film for children.

## What his story says to the rest of us

- **God was thinking of him first.** He did not go to jail looking for Jesus. "But God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8).
- **It began with one question from one unknown man.** Nobody knows Joshua's surname. He was a prisoner who asked his cellmate whether he believed in Jesus and then opened a Bible. "I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6). It is the same lesson as [the schoolteacher who led a fifteen-year-old David Oyedepo to Christ](/articles/betty-lasher-bishop-oyedepo/): the famous name is the harvest, and somebody else did the sowing.
- **Five million followers could not fill him.** "For what shall it profit a man, if he shall gain the whole world, and lose his own soul?" (Mark 8:36).
- **A convert needs a father.** By his own telling he met God in the cell and still went back to the world, because he came out with "no guidance" and "no example". An encounter with [the Holy Spirit](/holyspirit/) is a beginning. Somebody has to walk with the new believer afterwards.
- **Nobody is too far gone.** "This is a faithful saying, and worthy of all acceptation, that Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners; of whom I am chief" (1 Timothy 1:15).

## Questions this article answers

### What happened to Boonk Gang?

Instagram removed his account in July 2018 after he posted explicit videos, the same year he was arrested on weapons and drugs charges in California and appeared badly intoxicated in a filmed interview. He served about five months in the Los Angeles County Jail, by his own account, and there he says he met Christ. He now goes by Holy Gabbana.

### Is Boonk Gang a Christian now?

Yes, by his own public confession. He says a cellmate named Joshua taught him the Bible in jail and that he was touched by the Holy Spirit there. He went back to his old life for a time, posted a tearful video of repentance on 14 December 2020, and was baptised in February 2021.

### What is Boonk Gang's real name?

John Robert Hill Jr. He was born in 1996 and comes from Jacksonville, Florida. He dropped the name Boonk, became John Gabbana, and now preaches and makes Christian rap as Holy Gabbana.

### What is the film I Am Holy Gabbana about?

It is a 56-minute documentary of his life, directed by Jonathan Williams and first shown near Atlanta on 12 August 2022 as *I Am John Gabbana*. It follows him from a hard childhood through addiction and internet fame to jail and to faith in Christ.

### How did Holy Gabbana come to Christ in jail?

His cellmate asked him, "Do you believe in Jesus?" and then taught him the Bible. One night he sang a gospel song aloud in the cell, lay down weeping, and says the presence of God took his sadness away and filled him with joy.
