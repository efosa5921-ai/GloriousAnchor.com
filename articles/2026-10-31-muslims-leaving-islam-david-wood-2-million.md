## What David Wood argues

Wood's case rests on three things. First, his own inbox: during one month, he says, between one and three people a day wrote to tell him they had left Islam after watching his videos, and he receives such messages from every Muslim country. Second, the change the internet has made: for the first time in history, a Muslim in Lahore or Lagos can read the hadith, watch a debate and hear an ex-Muslim's testimony without a cleric in the room, and the questions that used to be unaskable are now a search away. Third, the gap between the public image and the private reality: Islam's leaders and the Western press both speak of the fastest-growing religion, while inside Muslim societies belief is quietly draining away.

The "nearly 2 million a year" headline is the clip channel's summary of that argument, not a figure from a survey, and Wood's own posts do not cite a source for it. Two million is about one in a thousand of the world's two billion Muslims. Whether that is too high or far too low depends on which numbers you trust.

## What the surveys say

**Islam as a whole is still growing fast, and switching is not why.** Pew Research's June 2025 report on the global religious landscape found the number of Muslims rose by 327 million between 2010 and 2020, against 122 million for Christians, almost entirely because Muslim families have more children and a younger population. Across the 117 countries Pew analysed, Muslims and Hindus were the groups least likely to gain or lose members by people changing religion. Anyone who tells you Islam is collapsing is reading a different report.

**But where people are free to answer, the leak is real.** Pew's March 2025 study of switching into and out of Islam in 13 countries found that in Bangladesh, Sri Lanka and most Muslim-majority countries, almost everyone raised Muslim still calls themselves Muslim. The exception is the United States, where only about three quarters of adults raised Muslim still identify that way: roughly one in four has left, most of them to no religion at all, though Pew also finds that converts into Islam in America roughly balance the losses. Those are the honest answers of people who will not be jailed or disowned for giving them.

**Iran is the case Wood leans on hardest, and it is startling.** In June 2020 the Netherlands-based group GAMAAN surveyed about 50,000 Iranians anonymously online, because no one in Iran will tell a government pollster the truth. Only about a third called themselves Shia Muslims. Twenty-two per cent said they had no religion, 9 per cent said they were atheists, and 1.5 per cent said they were Christians, which GAMAAN put at between 750,000 and 950,000 converts, almost all from Muslim homes, in a country where leaving Islam can carry a death sentence. Nearly half said they had moved from being religious to having no religion in their lifetime. The survey is online and self-selected, so it over-represents the educated and the young, but no Iranian official figure, which puts Muslims at 99 per cent, survives contact with it.

**The Arab world is cooling too.** Arab Barometer's 2019 survey of ten Arab countries, reported by the BBC under the headline "The Arab world in seven charts: are Arabs turning their backs on religion?", found the share describing themselves as "not religious" had risen from 8 to 13 per cent in six years, and to about a third in Tunisia, with the sharpest change among the under-30s.

**And then there are the claims nobody can check.** The most famous is the Libyan cleric Ahmad al-Qataani's lament on Al Jazeera in 2000 that six million African Muslims were becoming Christians every year, a figure quoted ever since and never substantiated. Open Doors describes Iran's underground church as one of the fastest-growing in the world. Mission agencies report more Muslims coming to Christ in the past thirty years than in the previous fourteen centuries, which is probably true and still unquantifiable.

Put together, the picture is this: Islam's total numbers are rising through births; its share of committed believers is falling wherever people can speak freely, dramatically so in Iran; and the number who formally leave each year is unknown, because in most of the Muslim world leaving is a crime. Two million a year is a guess. It may be modest.

The young are leaving fastest, the educated next, and the countries with the most repressive religious establishments, Iran above all, are losing most. ## Questions this article answers

### Are 2 million Muslims leaving Islam every year?

Nobody knows. The figure is the headline on a clip of David Wood's argument, not a survey result. Pew finds that Muslims are among the groups least likely to change religion worldwide, but that about one in four Americans raised Muslim has left, and an anonymous 2020 survey found only a third of Iranians still calling themselves Shia Muslims. In most Muslim countries leaving Islam is a crime, so true numbers cannot be counted.

### Is Islam growing or shrinking?

Growing in numbers: Pew counted 327 million more Muslims in 2020 than in 2010, almost entirely through births. But commitment is falling where people can answer freely, sharply among the young, the educated and in Iran.

### How many Iranians have left Islam?

GAMAAN's June 2020 survey of about 50,000 Iranians found about a third identifying as Shia, 22 per cent with no religion, 9 per cent atheist and 1.5 per cent Christian, which it estimated at 750,000 to 950,000 converts. Nearly half said they had lost their religion during their lifetime.

### Who is David Wood?

An American Christian apologist, born 1976, who co-founded Acts 17 Apologetics with the late Nabeel Qureshi and runs the Apologetics Roadshow channel. He became a Christian in prison in 1996 after a violent youth and has debated Muslim scholars for twenty years. In November 2025 he admitted to sexual encounters with two underage girls when he was 18, before his conversion.

### Why are Muslims leaving Islam?

Ex-Muslims most often cite reading Islam's own sources for themselves, violence committed in Islam's name, and an encounter with Jesus through a dream, a Bible, a friend or a video. The internet has made all three far more common.

### How should Christians respond to Muslims leaving Islam?

Pray for the harvest (Matthew 9:37-38), be ready to answer questions with gentleness (1 Peter 3:15), be honest about what the numbers do and do not show, and receive converts as family, since many lose their own.
