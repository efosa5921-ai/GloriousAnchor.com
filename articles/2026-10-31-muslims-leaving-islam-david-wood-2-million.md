## What David Wood sees from his inbox

Wood's vantage point is unusual. For two decades his debates and videos on Muhammad, the Quran and the hadith have been watched in every Muslim country, and the people who watch write to him. During one month, he has said, between one and three people a day messaged to tell him they had left Islam after watching his material. Those are only the ones who wrote, from only one channel.

His explanation is the internet. For fourteen centuries a Muslim who doubted had nowhere to take the doubt: the imam, the family and the law all stood between him and the sources. Now a young man in Cairo or Kano can read the hadith on his phone, watch a debate in which the sheikh does not win, and hear an ex-Muslim tell his story, all before breakfast and without anyone knowing. The questions that could not be asked are a search away. "Nearly 2 million a year" is the headline the clip channel put on his argument; Wood's point is that the real figure is unknowable and larger than anyone admits.

## Iran: the church that grew in the dark

Nowhere is the exodus plainer than Iran, where leaving Islam can carry a death sentence and the state says 99 per cent of the population is Muslim. In June 2020 the Netherlands-based research group GAMAAN asked about 50,000 Iranians anonymously online what they believed, because no Iranian will tell a government pollster the truth. Only a third said they were Shia Muslims. Twenty-two per cent said they had no religion, 9 per cent said they were atheists, and 1.5 per cent said they were Christians, which the researchers put at between 750,000 and 950,000 people, nearly all converts from Muslim homes. Nearly half said they had moved from being religious to having no religion in their own lifetime.

Open Doors calls the Iranian church the fastest-growing in the world. It meets in living rooms, its pastors serve prison terms for "acting against national security", and it keeps growing, fed by satellite television, Farsi Bibles and, over and over in the testimonies, dreams of a man in white who says He is Jesus. The regime that hanged converts in the 1990s has not stopped one of them in 2026.

## The Arab world is losing its religion

It is not only Iran. In 2019 Arab Barometer, the region's largest independent survey, found that the share of Arabs describing themselves as "not religious" had risen from 8 to 13 per cent in six years, and to about a third in Tunisia. The change was sharpest among the under-30s. The BBC's headline asked whether the Arab world was "turning its back on religion". In Algeria's Kabylie mountains, whole villages have churches now; in Egypt, the Coptic Church baptises converts it cannot name; in Saudi Arabia and the Gulf, ex-Muslim networks organise online under false names because the real ones would get them killed.

Among Muslims in the West, where answering honestly costs nothing, Pew Research finds that roughly one in four Americans raised Muslim no longer identifies as Muslim. That is the honest figure where honesty is free.

## Africa: "six million a year"

In December 2000 a Libyan cleric, Sheikh Ahmad al-Qataani, went on Al Jazeera and lamented that six million African Muslims were becoming Christians every year, "every hour 667 Muslims convert". Nobody has ever verified his figure, and Christians should not pretend to. But it is a Muslim cleric's own alarm, and the twenty-five years since have given him little comfort: sub-Saharan Africa is now the heartland of world Christianity, the Pentecostal churches of Nigeria, Ghana and Kenya send missionaries north, and across the Sahel men and women raised in Islam quietly join them, at a cost. Our own report on [Prophet Isa El-Buba](/articles/prophet-isa-el-buba-former-muslim-occultist-turned-prophet/), a former Muslim and occultist from Plateau State, is one such story among thousands. In northern Nigeria the convert's first question is not which church to join but who will shelter him when his family finds out.

## Why they come

Ask ex-Muslims why they left and three answers come back again and again. The first is reading Islam's own sources for themselves, Muhammad's life above all, which is why Wood's work matters. The second is the violence done in Islam's name, from the Islamic State's burnings to the killing of Christians in Nigeria, which drives thoughtful Muslims to ask what kind of God this is. The third, and the one that turns a doubter into a disciple, is Jesus Himself: a Bible given by a friend, a sentence in a debate, a Christian who did not hate them, and, in testimony after testimony from Tehran to Jos, a dream. Joel promised it: "your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions." The Lord is keeping that promise inside the house of Islam.

Nabeel Qureshi, who co-founded Acts 17 Apologetics with Wood after years of arguing with him as a devout Ahmadi Muslim, told that story in "Seeking Allah, Finding Jesus" before he died in 2017, and his book has carried more Muslims across than any statistic.

## Questions this article answers

### Are Muslims really leaving Islam?

Yes, every day, in every Muslim country, though exact numbers are impossible because leaving Islam is a crime in much of the Muslim world. David Wood estimates the exodus in the millions a year. An anonymous 2020 survey found only a third of Iranians still calling themselves Shia Muslims, and about one in four Americans raised Muslim has left.

### How many Iranians have become Christians?

GAMAAN's June 2020 survey of about 50,000 Iranians found 1.5 per cent identifying as Christians, which it estimated at 750,000 to 950,000 converts, and Open Doors calls Iran's underground church the fastest-growing in the world.

### Why are Muslims leaving Islam?

Ex-Muslims most often cite reading Islam's own sources for themselves, violence committed in Islam's name, and a personal encounter with Jesus, often through a dream, a Bible or a Christian friend. The internet has made all three far more common.

### What does David Wood say about Muslims leaving Islam?

That the exodus is far larger than Muslim leaders or Western media admit, that the internet has made Islam's problems impossible to hide, and that he hears from people who have left Islam every day. His video is embedded above.

### Do Muslims dream about Jesus?

Dreams and visions of Jesus are among the most common reasons former Muslims give for seeking Him, reported from Iran to Nigeria, and Scripture promises exactly that in Joel 2:28.

### How should Christians respond to Muslims leaving Islam?

Pray for the harvest (Matthew 9:37-38), be ready to answer questions with gentleness (1 Peter 3:15), expect the nations to come (Isaiah 19:25, Revelation 7:9), and receive converts as family, since many lose their own.
