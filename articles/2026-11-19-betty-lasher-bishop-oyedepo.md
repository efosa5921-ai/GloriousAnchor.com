> *And they that be wise shall shine as the brightness of the firmament; and they that turn many to righteousness as the stars for ever and ever.* (Daniel 12:3)

On Wednesday 19 February 1969, in a boys' secondary school in a small town in what is now Kwara State, Nigeria, an American woman of thirty-eight led a fifteen-year-old pupil to Christ. She was his teacher. She was unmarried, far from home, and unknown outside the school. The boy was David Olaniyi Oyedepo.

Most people who know his name do not know hers. He never forgot it. This is the story of Betty Lasher, of the boy she "got hold of", and of what one hidden life can set in motion.

*Cover: Bishop Oyedepo's words about Betty Lasher, spoken on the Sunday after her death in August 2022.*

## Who was Betty Lasher?

Betty Lasher was born on 18 June 1930 and came from Portland, Oregon, in the United States. Her father was a pastor, she told her pupils. She never married. She went to Nigeria as a missionary teacher and taught Christian Religious Knowledge at Government Secondary School, Omu-Aran, then an all-boys school.

The fullest picture of her comes from one of those boys. Dr Femi Emmanuel, now a lecturer at the West Africa Theological Seminary in Lagos, told Church Times Nigeria in 2020 what her classroom was like:

- **A second library.** "She had a rich library of books which was possibly richer than the school library. So students would go to read in her library and she took time to mentor many students that came her way."
- **A discipleship class in her office.** "She had a large office to herself where she ran a discipleship class. So apart from teaching CRK, she had sessions with students regularly, mentoring them and leading them to Christ."
- **A Datsun on Sunday evenings.** She lived far from the school compound, and fetched pupils in her car for the Sunday evening fellowship and drove them back afterwards.
- **More than the syllabus.** "I learnt how to draw the map of Palestine, how to lead people to Christ and several other things from her." She taught them to draw the tabernacle of the Old Testament.
- **No soft touch.** She was "a disciplinarian", and Dr Emmanuel remembers being on the wrong end of it. She also spoke a little Yoruba.

"When I look back, I wonder at the life of sacrifice that she lived," he said. "She came to Nigeria as a young lady and stayed all through the years in that village."

Accounts of her dates differ. Her online memorial gives her years of missionary teaching as 1967 to 1991; Dr Emmanuel remembers her serving more than thirty years and leaving Omu-Aran in 1990. Other former students say she served with SIM, the mission whose churches became ECWA, and taught afterwards at ECWA Theological College, Kagoro, in Kaduna State, before going home to Oregon in the late 1990s. What nobody disputes is that she gave Nigeria the best decades of her life.

## 19 February 1969: how Bishop Oyedepo was born again

David Oyedepo was born on 27 September 1954 in Osogbo and comes from Omu-Aran. His father was a Muslim healer, his mother belonged to the Cherubim and Seraphim church, and he was brought up by his grandmother, who he says "made a man out of me as a child". He was a churchgoing boy. He was not yet a converted one.

He told journalists the rest himself on his sixtieth birthday in 2014: "I had a missionary teacher while still in secondary school called Betty Lasher. She communicated the love of God with such passion and led me to Christ on February 19, 1969, when I was only 15 years old."

In June 2020, when she turned ninety, he described what it had been like to be her pupil: "You could catch love on her face. She loved kids. The love of Jesus radiating from her eyeballs, she caught me with that love and several others." And he added: "Many notable sons came out of that woman. Sweet mummy."

He was not the only one. The school's Fellowship of Christian Students called her its matriarch, and by his account her old boys include ministers of the gospel, professors and businessmen.

## What that one conversion became

Twelve years after that February, in May 1981, the young man had the eighteen-hour vision he calls his mandate, and founded what is now the Living Faith Church Worldwide, known as Winners' Chapel. Its Faith Tabernacle at Ota seats 50,400 and holds the Guinness record for the largest church auditorium; a larger building, The Ark, is rising beside it. The church has congregations in several hundred cities on four continents, and has founded two universities, Covenant University at Ota and Landmark University.

Landmark University stands in Omu-Aran. The boy who was led to Christ in that town's secondary school built a university there.

Earlier this month it was Bishop Oyedepo who dedicated the [Hand of God Cathedral](/articles/hand-of-god-cathedral-salvation-ministries-port-harcourt/) in Port Harcourt, for a church whose founder, David Ibiyeomie, calls the bishop his father in the faith. The line runs from her classroom through him to men she never met.

## "Sweet mother": what Oyedepo said when she died

Betty Lasher died at about eight in the evening, American time, on Friday 26 August 2022, aged 92, with her nephews and nieces around her and one of her old boys from Omu-Aran, Dr David Bola Dada, at her bedside. The notice sent to her former pupils said the news came "with a shout of victory", and ended: "Death is swallowed up in victory."

Two days later Bishop Oyedepo told his congregation:

> "My mother in the Lord went home two nights ago. Betty Lasher. I sobbed. I rose and wrote a poem. 53 years ago she changed my life. I spoke with her last month."

He said he had told her: "You have left an army behind who are following Jesus dedicatedly as you did. A number of them great ministers of the gospel, a number of them professors, big time businessmen who are still following Jesus with all their heart."

Then he drew the lesson for the people in front of him: "Engage your heart in all you do. She got hold of me at 15 and I have still not forgotten."

And this, which is the heart of the matter:

> "If I don't know anything, I know sweet mommy is in her mansion. I know she has a crown right now, because if I wasn't saved, I won't be in ministry. Whatever people got saved here will also be credited to her account. That is what happens to people you are leading to Christ."

## The fruit that abounds to her account

That last sentence is not sentiment. It is Paul's own bookkeeping. He told the Philippians he wanted "fruit that may abound to your account" (Philippians 4:17), and told the Corinthians: "I have planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase" (1 Corinthians 3:6). Jesus said that "he that reapeth receiveth wages, and gathereth fruit unto life eternal: that both he that soweth and he that reapeth may rejoice together" (John 4:36).

The history of the church is full of this. A Sunday-school teacher named Edward Kimball walked into a Boston shoe shop in 1855 and led a young clerk to Christ; the clerk was D. L. Moody. [Billy Graham](/articles/billy-graham-gods-general/) was converted at sixteen under the preaching of an evangelist few remember, Mordecai Ham. The famous name is the harvest. Somebody else did the sowing.

Betty Lasher did not found a church, write a book or fill a stadium. She taught Scripture to schoolboys in a town most of the world could not find on a map, kept a library, ran a fellowship, drove a Datsun on Sunday evenings, and stayed. She did not live to be famous. She lived to be faithful, and God did the multiplying. "Who hath despised the day of small things?" (Zechariah 4:10).

There is a word here for every teacher, every Sunday-school worker, every parent and grandparent, and every believer who thinks their corner too small to matter. Nigeria's Pentecostal churches were themselves [a small and despised company in 1977](/articles/festac-77-nigeria-idolatry-repentance/), and look what God did with them. The boy in front of you may be the one. You will probably never know in this life. She very nearly did not: it was half a century before the world heard her name, and then only because a grateful man kept saying it.

## Questions this article answers

### Who led Bishop Oyedepo to Christ?

Betty Lasher, an American missionary who taught Christian Religious Knowledge at Government Secondary School, Omu-Aran, Kwara State. Bishop Oyedepo has said she "communicated the love of God with such passion" and led him to Christ when he was her pupil.

### When was Bishop David Oyedepo born again?

On 19 February 1969, at the age of fifteen, by his own account. He was a secondary-school pupil in Omu-Aran at the time, and received the vision he calls his mandate twelve years later, in May 1981.

### Who was Betty Lasher?

She was born on 18 June 1930, came from Portland, Oregon, and was the daughter of a pastor. She never married, spent decades as a missionary teacher in Nigeria, ran a discipleship class and a library for her pupils, and was called the matriarch of her school's Fellowship of Christian Students.

### When did Betty Lasher die, and what did Bishop Oyedepo say?

She died on 26 August 2022, aged 92. Two days later Bishop Oyedepo called her "my mother in the Lord", said he sobbed and wrote a poem, and told his church: "Whatever people got saved here will also be credited to her account."

### What does the Bible say about those who lead others to Christ?

That their reward is shared with the harvest they began. "They that turn many to righteousness" shall shine "as the stars for ever and ever" (Daniel 12:3), and the sower and the reaper "rejoice together" (John 4:36).
