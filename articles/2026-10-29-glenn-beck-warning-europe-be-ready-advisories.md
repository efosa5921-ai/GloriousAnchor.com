> *You also must be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect.* (Luke 12:40)

"The last time I felt the urge to give a warning like this was in 2007, right before the 2008 crash." That is how Glenn Beck opened a video posted to X on Tuesday 28 October 2026 that has since been watched millions of times. "Across Europe, governments are quietly issuing emergency 'Be Ready' advisories, asking citizens to stockpile cash, water, and food. Add that to the Pentagon's new FORTRESS initiative and it sure looks like... SOMETHING is coming. They're preparing for impact. And you should too. I hope and pray I'm wrong. But I think massive global conflict is coming."

Beck is a broadcaster, not a prophet, and he says so himself: some of his past warnings have come true and others have not. But the two things he points to are real, and many of our readers have asked about them. This article lays out what European governments are actually telling their citizens, why they say they are doing it, how it fits with the Pentagon plan we [reported on yesterday](/articles/pentagon-fortress-america-90-day-doomsday-plan/), where Beck's reading goes beyond the evidence, and what "be ready" means for a Christian.

## What Britain's "Be Ready" campaign actually says

The advisory that set Beck off is British. On Wednesday 22 October the Cabinet Office launched "Be Ready", described as the first stage of a three-year public awareness campaign, with a new website replacing the "Prepare" site the previous government put up in May 2024. It asks every household that can afford it to be able to look after itself for three days if electricity, water, phone networks or the internet go down. The checklist is modest:

- about three litres of drinking water per person per day, so nine litres each for three days, plus some for washing;
- food that needs no fridge and no cooking;
- essential medicines and a first-aid kit;
- a torch, a battery or wind-up radio, and a charged power bank;
- cash, because when the card machines are down a cash machine is no use either;
- copies of important documents, blankets and warm clothing, and a simple family plan.

The campaign's own wording is: "From severe weather to cyber attacks, emergencies can disrupt the services you rely on. Being ready for three days helps you manage while support reaches those in need." Security Minister Dan Jarvis said the aim was to "reduce pressure on emergency services" and to bring Britain "into line with European counterparts, where advice like this is a normal part of everyday life." Defence Secretary Wes Streeting urged people to be "alert, not alarmed," pointing to flooding, power cuts, burst pipes and "increasingly sophisticated cyber attacks" on things like supermarket payment systems. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, asked about it on the radio, said: "It's not about panicking. It's about just taking sensible measures to have enough in the house to tide you over," and admitted his own cupboard holds "plenty" of beans and soup.

The public listened. Within days Ocado had run out of several kinds of bottled water, beans and soup; Iceland reported a 40 per cent jump in tinned-food sales; Maplin said torch sales nearly quadrupled and radio sales almost doubled in three days. Two storms hit the country the same weekend, which The National noted would have lifted those sales anyway.

## What the rest of Europe says

Britain is late to this, not early, which is the first thing to notice about Beck's "quietly". The others have been loud for two years.

- **The European Union.** In March 2025 the Commission's Preparedness Union Strategy asked all 450 million citizens to be able to manage for 72 hours without help, naming war, cyber attacks, climate disasters and disease as the reasons. In March 2026 Commissioner Hadja Lahbib repeated the call, noting that six in ten Europeans still said they were not ready.
- **Germany.** The federal government's civil-protection advice is the most demanding: supplies for ten days, including two litres of liquid per person per day. Its website says plainly that "in addition to war scenarios, there are also numerous peacetime crises" such as animal disease or industrial accidents that could empty the shelves.
- **Sweden.** The Swedish Civil Defence and Resilience Agency posted a booklet, "If Crisis or War Comes", to every household in November 2024 and asks families to manage for a week. Its language is the frankest in Europe: "War is being waged in our corner of the world and the security situation is the most serious since the Second World War. An armed attack against Sweden cannot be ruled out."
- **France, the Netherlands and Ireland** all work to the 72-hour standard, and Britain says it consulted all three. France's survival manual lists food, water, medicines, a radio, a torch, cash, copies of documents, spare keys and warm clothes. Finland, Norway and Denmark have run similar campaigns.
- **The cash.** Beck is right that central banks are telling people to hold notes. The European Central Bank's September 2025 paper "Keep calm and carry cash" suggests €70 to €100 per person, enough for about 72 hours; the Dutch central bank says €70 per adult and €30 per child; Austria's says €100 per household member; the Bank of Finland says enough for 72 hours; and in March 2026 Sweden's Riksbank asked every adult to keep 1,000 kronor, about 110 dollars, at home. The reason given in every case is the same: a cyber attack or a power failure that takes the payment system down.

So Beck's first claim is true and understated: it is not only Europe. But the second word in his sentence, "quietly", is wrong. These advisories were launched at press conferences, posted through letterboxes and debated on breakfast radio.

## Why they are doing it

Every one of these governments gives the same three reasons, in the same order: severe weather, cyber attacks on the systems that run daily life, and, further down the list, war. Britain's National Audit Office warned in September that the food supply chain is exposed to both hackers and climate; a cold-storage industry chief said Russian hackers "know that there are pinch points in the food supply system." Sweden and Germany name war outright. Britain's ministers, asked about Russia and Iran, speak of "hostile activity" and leave it there.

Underneath all of it is a change of philosophy. For two generations Western governments told citizens that in an emergency the state would come. Now they are saying, in effect, that for the first three days it may not, because the emergency services will be busy with the people who cannot help themselves. That is a sober admission, and it is the real news in all of this. It is not a coded announcement of an imminent war.

## Where FORTRESS fits

The Pentagon side of Beck's warning is the FORTRESS America initiative, announced on 30 September by Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth, which aims to make major military bases in the United States able to run for a sustained period with no outside power, water or fuel, with portable reactors and stockpiles. Fox News reported internal documents setting a target of 90 days; the Pentagon confirmed the programme but not the number. We examined it in detail yesterday: [what FORTRESS America and the 90-day rule really say](/articles/pentagon-fortress-america-90-day-doomsday-plan/).

Put the two together and Beck's logic is simple: civilians are being told to last three days, soldiers ninety, therefore someone expects the lights to go out. The honest answer is that someone does expect the lights might go out, and has said so, repeatedly, in public. Whether a Russian cyber attack on a power grid, a storm, a Middle East war that closes the Gulf, or something nobody has thought of, the planners' job is to assume it and prepare. Preparing for a thing is not the same as knowing it is coming. Even the Dallas Express, reporting Beck's video sympathetically, added that "neither initiative independently establishes that war is imminent."

## What Beck gets right, and where he goes further than the evidence

He is right that the pattern is real, that it is new, and that it is global: Britain, the EU, Germany, the Nordic countries, the central banks and the Pentagon have all moved in the same direction within eighteen months, and that is worth noticing. He is right that ordinary households should take the official advice, which is cheap and sensible. And he is right to say "I hope and pray I'm wrong."

He goes further than the evidence when he reads the advisories as a secret signal of "massive global conflict". Governments have told us what they fear, and war is on the list but not at the top of it. His 2007 comparison is a feeling, not a fact; he was right about 2008 and wrong about several things since, as he acknowledges. A Christian reading the news should hold both halves: the preparations are real, and the conclusion is a guess.

## How a Christian should "be ready"

It is striking that the slogan every government has settled on is one the Lord used first. "You also must be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect" (Luke 12:40). Jesus was not talking about tinned beans, but the posture He commands is the one these campaigns are borrowing: awake, provisioned, unafraid.

So, first, take the practical advice, because Scripture commends it. "The prudent sees danger and hides himself, but the simple go on and suffer for it" (Proverbs 22:3). Joseph stored grain for seven years before the famine (Genesis 41). Three days of water, some tins, a torch, a little cash and a plan for how your family would find each other is not fear; it is stewardship, and it frees you to help a neighbour who did not prepare.

Second, refuse the fear. Jesus told us in advance that there would be "wars and rumours of wars" and added, "see that you are not alarmed" (Matthew 24:6). The righteous man "is not afraid of bad news; his heart is firm, trusting in the LORD" (Psalm 112:7). A stocked cupboard is no substitute for that trust, and a video that leaves you anxious has served you worse than one that leaves you prepared.

Third, be ready in the way that matters most. The same Peter who tells believers to "always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have" (1 Peter 3:15) is describing a readiness no government can issue. If the pattern Beck sees does turn into something, the people around you will be asking why you are calm. Have the answer ready. And whether or not anything comes, the Lord is: "when these things begin to take place, straighten up and raise your heads, because your redemption is drawing near" (Luke 21:28).

## Questions this article answers

### What did Glenn Beck warn about on 28 October 2026?

In a video on X he said that European governments were "quietly" issuing "Be Ready" advisories telling citizens to stockpile cash, water and food, that together with the Pentagon's FORTRESS initiative this meant "something is coming", and that he believed "massive global conflict" was coming, adding, "I hope and pray I'm wrong."

### Is the UK government telling people to stockpile?

Yes, modestly. The "Be Ready" campaign launched on 22 October 2026 asks households to be able to manage for three days: about three litres of water per person per day, food that needs no cooking, medicines, a torch, a radio, a power bank and some cash. Ministers say it is for storms, power cuts and cyber attacks and urge people to be "alert, not alarmed."

### Which other countries have told citizens to prepare?

The EU asked all citizens for a 72-hour kit in March 2025. Germany advises ten days of supplies, Sweden a week, and France, the Netherlands, Ireland, Finland, Norway and Denmark run similar campaigns. The European Central Bank, the Dutch, Austrian, Finnish and Swedish central banks all advise keeping cash at home.

### How much cash should you keep at home?

The European Central Bank suggests €70 to €100 per person; the Dutch central bank €70 per adult and €30 per child; Austria €100 per household member; Sweden's Riksbank 1,000 kronor per adult. Britain's checklist says "cash" without a figure.

### Does the Be Ready campaign mean war is coming?

No government has said so. Britain names severe weather and cyber attacks first; Sweden and Germany name war among several possibilities. The campaigns show that governments expect that in a serious emergency help may take days to arrive, which is a real change, but preparing for a danger is not the same as knowing it is imminent.

### What does the Bible say about being ready?

Jesus commands it: "You also must be ready, for the Son of Man is coming at an hour you do not expect" (Luke 12:40). Scripture commends prudent provision (Proverbs 22:3, Genesis 41) and forbids fear (Matthew 24:6, Psalm 112:7), and tells believers to be ready to explain their hope (1 Peter 3:15).
