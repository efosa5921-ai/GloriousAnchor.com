> *And you will hear of wars and rumours of wars. See that you are not alarmed, for this must take place, but the end is not yet.* (Matthew 24:6)

"The Pentagon's Doomsday Plan" is how a widely shared video on X summed it up this week: portable reactors, 90-day stockpiles and something called the Fortress initiative, as America braces for a war that reaches its own soil. The video drew on a report by Fox News Digital that has since been picked up around the world, and the phrase "doomsday plan" has travelled with it.

Some of this is real and new. Some of it is a familiar programme wearing a frightening name. This article separates the two, and then asks what a Christian should make of a government planning for the lights to go out.

## What FORTRESS America is

FORTRESS stands for Federal Operations for Resilience, Troop Resources, Energy, Supply and Survivability. Secretary of War Pete Hegseth announced it on 30 September in a "State of the Force" address to service members at Quantico, Virginia, and signed a memo the same day. He called it "a national insurance policy for the American homeland".

His own words set out the aim: "Every major military installation in America is going to generate its own power independent of the civilian grid. If an adversary takes down the grid by cyber attack, by sabotage or by any means, our bases stay lit. Our aircraft keep flying. Our command centers never go dark."

The memo lists five pillars: energy independence; resilient supply chains and a domestic industrial base; a strategic defence reserve; hardened installations and cyber resilience; and resilience in space and the radio spectrum. Hegseth spoke of bringing "the rare earths, the specialty metals, the semiconductors, the propellants" home, of reserves "deep enough to fight through disruption. Think munitions, fuel, medicine, blood," and of hardening every base "against cyber intrusion, sabotage and drones."

He also said what the name does not mean: "A fortress is not a place where you hide from. It's a ground you stand on, the foundation you fight from."

A programme office was ordered within thirty days. On 6 October Under Secretary Michael Duffey named Richard "Matt" O'Malley its interim director, with a two-star general as military adviser, and told eight senior officials to assign staff to it. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell, by several accounts the man who conceived the plan, has become its public voice.

## Where the "90 days" comes from

On 25 October Fox News Digital reported, citing internal planning documents, that under FORTRESS every major military installation in the continental United States would be expected to sustain essential operations for at least 90 days without outside support, including electricity, water, fuel and communications. The means listed were portable nuclear reactors, large-scale battery storage, microgrids that can cut a base off from the civilian grid, reserves of munitions, fuel, critical minerals, spare parts and medical supplies, two independent supply routes for every critical dependency, and a network of civilian factories pre-cleared to switch to defence production.

That is the "doomsday plan". Note what it is and is not. The Pentagon has not published a 90-day requirement; the figure rests on documents Fox reviewed. Parnell shared the report on X but, as the fact-checking site Snopes noted in rating the claim "mostly true", he "has not confirmed the 90-day requirement". The report gives no timeline and no cost. A senior defence official told Air and Space Forces Magazine the department is still assessing roughly 500 bases in the continental United States and that finishing the job could take years, even decades; the magazine's own estimate ran to tens of billions of dollars, possibly hundreds. And nobody has said a war is coming: the Pentagon has named no country and no date.

## Why they are doing it

Parnell has explained the thinking without apology. "Power is combat now. Without electricity, you lose communications, water, logistics, assembly lines and just basic military command structures. We don't want to rely on standard civilian municipal infrastructure to run our national defense. We just simply can't. It's an intolerable risk."

And: "The next conflict will likely be fought on multiple fronts. Look at the geopolitics behind any major war. When that happens, global supply chains will break." He has said the old assumption "that two oceans would protect us" is gone, and that "you don't want to be making these decisions in the middle of a global multi-front conflict. We have to have a plan."

The threats he has in mind are not imaginary. In 2024 American agencies warned that Chinese state hackers, known as Volt Typhoon, had been sitting inside the networks that run water, energy and transport for years, positioned to disrupt them in a crisis. China has restricted exports of the metals that chips and weapons depend on. Small drones have shown in Ukraine what they can do to a power substation. The War Zone's analysis points out that most American bases still draw their power from the same ageing civilian grid as the towns around them, and that the microreactors meant to change that, under the Army's Janus programme, are not due until 2028 and have yet to be proven at scale.

## Britain is telling families the same thing

Six days before the Fox report, the British government launched "Be Ready", a public campaign asking every household to be able to cope for three days without help: three litres of drinking water per person per day, food that needs no cooking, a torch, a power bank, a first-aid kit, a wind-up radio, cash in case the cards stop working. The Defence Secretary wrote that adversaries could attack the country by 2030, "a little over 1,000 days" away, and that "to prevent the worst, we have to prepare for it." Britain's cyber security centre logged more than two hundred serious incidents in a year, three-quarters linked to states, chiefly Russia, China and Iran. The European Union told its citizens in 2025 to keep 72 hours of supplies.

So the Pentagon's plan is one piece of a pattern. Western governments have decided, out loud, that a war in which the home front loses power and water is possible, and they are telling their soldiers, and now their citizens, to be ready for it.

## How a Christian should read it

Three things, held together.

**Do not be alarmed.** Jesus said that his people would hear of wars and rumours of wars, and he gave one instruction: "See that you are not alarmed, for this must take place, but the end is not yet" (Matthew 24:6). A government planning for disruption is not a sign that the world ends next spring. It is a sign that governments are doing what governments are for. Fear sells videos; it does not fit a people whose "God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble" (Psalm 46:1).

**Do not be naive either.** "The prudent sees danger and hides himself, but the simple go on and suffer for it" (Proverbs 22:3). Joseph filled Egypt's storehouses in the fat years because God showed him lean ones were coming (Genesis 41), and his prudence fed nations. Keeping water, food, light and a little cash for a few days is not prophecy-fever; it is the same wisdom Britain is now asking of every household, and the church that is ready can serve its neighbours when the lights go out instead of queueing with them.

**Keep your trust where it belongs.** America's bases may one day run on their own reactors; "some trust in chariots and some in horses, but we trust in the name of the LORD our God" (Psalm 20:7). Hegseth called FORTRESS an insurance policy. The Christian already has one, and it covers more than ninety days: "He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all, how will he not also with him graciously give us all things?" (Romans 8:32).

Those who watch these developments with Bible prophecy in mind will note that Scripture describes a time when "nation will rise against nation" and men's hearts fail "for fear and for expectation of the things which are coming on the earth" (Luke 21:10, 26). The fear is foretold; so is the answer: "When these things begin to take place, straighten up and raise your heads, because your redemption is drawing near" (Luke 21:28).

## Questions this article answers

### What is the Pentagon's "doomsday plan"?

It is the internet's name for FORTRESS America, a Pentagon initiative announced by Pete Hegseth on 30 September 2026 to make US military bases independent of the civilian power grid, bring supply chains home, build strategic reserves, harden bases against cyber attack, sabotage and drones, and protect satellites and radio spectrum. The "doomsday" label came after Fox News reported on 25 October that bases would be expected to run for 90 days without outside support.

### Is the 90-day requirement confirmed?

Not officially. Fox News Digital reported it from internal planning documents. Spokesman Sean Parnell shared the report but has not confirmed the figure, and the Pentagon has published no directive, timeline or cost. Snopes rates the claim "mostly true": the initiative is real, the 90-day detail is reported rather than announced.

### What does FORTRESS America include?

Five pillars: energy independence (microgrids, batteries and portable nuclear reactors so bases can disconnect from the civilian grid); supply chain and industrial base resilience; a strategic defence reserve of munitions, fuel, medicine and blood; installation hardening and cyber resilience; and resilience in space and spectrum. A programme office under interim director Matt O'Malley is building the implementation plan.

### Why is the Pentagon preparing for a war on American soil?

Officials say the assumption that two oceans protect the United States no longer holds. Chinese hackers have been found inside the networks that run American water and power; China controls key minerals; drones and sabotage can hit a grid. Parnell: "Power is combat now... It's an intolerable risk" to run national defence on civilian infrastructure. No officials have said a war is imminent or named an enemy.

### Is Britain telling people to stockpile?

Yes. The UK's "Be Ready" campaign, launched on 22 October 2026, asks every household to be able to manage three days without help: three litres of water per person per day, no-cook food, a torch, power bank, first-aid kit, wind-up radio and cash. The EU gave similar 72-hour advice in 2025.

### How should Christians respond to news like this?

Without alarm (Matthew 24:6; Psalm 46:1), without naivety (Proverbs 22:3; Genesis 41), and with trust in God rather than in reactors or stockpiles (Psalm 20:7; Romans 8:32). Sensible household preparedness is wisdom, not panic, and a prepared church can serve its neighbours in a crisis.
